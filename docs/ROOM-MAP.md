# The room map

Generated by `scripts/classify/build-room-map.py` from a corpus of **433,449** papers.
Room ceiling **10,000** — **provisional**: D9 (LANE-CURVE) has not run, so this is the brief's placeholder, not a measured ceiling. Re-run this script with `--max-room N` once `docs/HAYSTACK-CURVE.md` exists.

A **room** is a scoped haystack a subject agent searches. The bet is that our stack wins inside
small rooms (see `docs/BEDROCK-KB-BAKEOFF.md`).
This is **not** a query-time router — the user's navigation choice is the router.

## Rooms and shards are different things

- A **room** is what a user picks. Always topical, always choosable.
- A **shard** is what the index holds. **The ceiling governs shards, not rooms.**

An earlier version of this map put the year band in the room *name*, producing four
"Neuroscience · Brain" rooms separated only by date. Navigation is the router, and a user's
question has no date — so they could not choose correctly, and a route the user has no basis
for choosing fails **invisibly**, which is the exact failure a scoped architecture exists to
prevent. Year bands were never wrong as a *sharding* strategy, only as a *naming* one.

Picking "Neuroscience · Brain" searches all four of its shards: the user is still confined to
21,287 documents instead of 433,449, and every shard stays under the ceiling.

### What the predicted labels bought

Both configurations place every paper, so "papers without a room" is zero either way and
says nothing. The real gain is **which axis** a paper is placed on: a room split by MeSH
subtree is a topic a researcher can choose; a room split by year is a filing cabinet.

| axis | papers, observed MeSH only | papers, + predicted | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mesh` | 140,664 | 282,801 | +142,137 |
| `category` | 95,142 | 95,142 | +0 |
| `no-mesh-facet` | 102,205 | 7,711 | -94,494 |
| `residual` | 508 | 508 | +0 |

Rooms rose 160 → 247: papers that previously fell into a few
huge undifferentiated year buckets now separate into named topics.

## Calibration — predicted labels are not observed labels

261,768 papers carry a **predicted** MeSH tag from `knn-bge-m3-k15` rather than an
NLM-assigned one. They live in a separate table (`preprint_mesh_predicted`), every row carries
its probability and the model id, and observed MeSH is never overwritten.

**The labeller is kNN propagation over stock bge-m3 embeddings, NOT cshl-MESH-tagger.**
The tagger was trained, scored, and lost to that baseline on every ranking metric
(P@5 58.68 vs 63.39, R@15 52.66 vs 61.82 on the full held-out test split), so using it here
would have meant labelling the corpus with the arm measured to be worse. Both arms are
loaded in `preprint_mesh_predicted`, distinguished by `model`, so the choice is reversible
and auditable. The `predicted` column
below is how many of a room's members were placed using a predicted label — a room at 100% is a
room whose very existence is a model's opinion, and should be read that way.

## Papers without a room: **zero**

Every paper in the corpus is in exactly one room. Rooms are a partition, not an overlay —
so this count is meaningful rather than an artefact of double-counting.

## Cross-category rooms (stretch)

Category is author-assigned at submission — a filing decision, not a claim about what a paper
is near. Where two categories genuinely overlap, a category-first partition cuts one community
in half. This section proposes unions that the **cshl-CITE** encoder
(`preprint_embeddings_cshl`) says are one room.

### Centroid similarity alone is not evidence — and that is the finding

**19,439** cross-category room pairs sit at centroid cosine ≥ 0.8, and the
top candidates are packed between 0.986 and 0.990 — the measure barely separates them. But their
**interleaving** (how often a paper's nearest neighbour *inside the union* comes from the other
room) ranges from 0.17 to 0.42 across that same band. Centroid proximity would happily have
married rooms that are two distinct blobs sitting near the corpus mean.

### Limits, stated rather than left implicit

- **19,439 candidate pairs existed; 12 were tested** — the top of the centroid-cosine
  ranking, which I then showed is a weak proxy for real overlap. These are therefore *not*
  necessarily the best 12 merges. A sampled probe, not an exhaustive search.
- Only pairs whose union fits the ceiling were considered, so large-scale overlaps are excluded
  by construction.
- Interleaving is measured on 25 sampled papers per side. It ranks candidates; it carries no
  confidence interval.
- **Nothing here is applied.** The map ships as the category-first partition. These are
  proposals for a human to accept or reject.
